Dead Reckoning

Bridgetown, Barbados, March 1745. You are master of the brig Hesper, 160 tons. The trade wind blows steady from the east. Choose your orders.

Your ship

Free sailing has no end: anchor at any port to trade. Sea trials show your true position so you can learn the ship. On the Lost at sea voyages the chart shows only your own reckoning.

Save and load

The game also saves itself about every 45 seconds while you sail. Loading replaces the voyage you are on, and the ship starts paused so you can look around first.

How to sail the Hesper

Wind is everything. A square-rigged brig cannot sail closer than about 6 points (67°) to the wind. The shaded wedge on the compass is where she will stall. She is fastest with the wind on her quarter, a broad reach.

To go upwind, tack. Sail close-hauled on one side, then put the helm over in 5° steps (A and D, or the buttons) to swing her bows through the wind onto the other side. Keep speed up first: if she slows too much in the turn she will miss stays and fall back. Wearing turns the long way round through downwind; it is slower but never fails.

Trim the yards. The compass shows your brig from above with her two yards. The dashed line is the best angle for this wind. Brace the yards to match it: the sail edge turns green when they draw well, amber or red when they don't. With the wind behind you the yards go square; close-hauled they are braced right round. The crew braces them for you when you tack or wear. Tick the bosun box to have them trimmed always.

Time. 1× is real time: one second on the clock for each real second, so a tack takes about half a minute and a Channel-sized crossing takes hours. Use 3× to hurry a little, or 60× (one minute per second) to skip across open water. The Skip 3 h button jumps three hours ahead; it stops early if something needs you. The game slows to 60× when something needs you.

Mind your canvas. More sail is faster until the wind gets up. If the rigging strains, shorten sail, or it will be damaged and she will sail slower.

Lost at sea. Your pencil track comes from the course you ordered and the speed read off the log line. The helmsman wanders, the ship slips to leeward and the current sets you west, and none of that is in your pencil track. The dashed ellipse shows how far off you might be. Take a noon sight for latitude, and cross-bearings on two peaks for a full fix.
